The market variables do not just fail to explain the bad period — they point the wrong way

The diagnostic characterised the period from market data alone, never from strategy P&L, so the answer could not be circular. Seven variables, session by session, across the full development window. The period should have been better than average. It was the worst on record. It is also not a multivariate outlier (93.7th percentile, below the 95th-percentile bar) and not novel — its nearest analog is 2025-04-25 → 2025-09-19, and that holds at every window length from 40 to 180 sessions. Every “the regime turned” reading is now excluded, not merely unsupported. The honest caveat: seven variables are a thin instrument, and this is a statement about these variables.

The incumbent is degraded in that window too — so last wave’s two failures were low-power, not adverse

This had never been checked, and it has the widest consequences of anything here. In the same 106 sessions: Solar leg Sharpe −0.387, BEST_ONE_NQ +0.073, ES −1.046, YM −1.020. Challenger comparisons inside that window were being made against a reference that was itself broken — a power problem, not evidence that the challengers are fragile.

Two things corroborate it. M1, last wave’s failure, was actually helping between the mid-2024 market break and the stub (+$8,073). And YM — the instrument whose negative sign decided M5’s 2-of-3 count — runs +$20,066 over that same interval. That sign count was never a stable property of the instrument set. The P&L reconstruction behind this table is what the reviewer most wanted to break; it reconciles exactly to both committed NinjaTrader nets.

Its own review withdrew two of five headlines — and one was true by construction

The changepoint procedure excluded the outer 10% of the sample as candidate break locations, for a legitimate statistical reason. With 1,139 sessions that puts the last admissible break at index 1,024. The 2026 window starts at index 1,033. “No changepoint in 2026” could not have come out any other way. The conclusion survives — re-running with 2% and 5% exclusions leaves every estimate unchanged — but the reason given was not the reason, and evaluated at the 2026 boundary three of the tests would in fact fire.

Also withdrawn: “a monotone trend, not a break.” A model comparison prefers a step over a trend for both relevant variables, and my own yearly table — printed two lines below the word “monotonically” — shows both of them rising into 2023. An index bug moved the estimated market boundary from 2024-07-09 to 2024-08-05, and dropping any single variable moves it by up to 545 days against a 49-day confidence interval, so it is weakly identified and the successor spec has been told to treat it as a candidate, never as authoritative.

Two smaller corrections worth your attention

Product A’s resilience in that window is not diversification. I attributed it to the 60/40 blend. Decomposed: roughly $7,243 of it is the short-halving overlay — a fitted in-sample constant — against $1,721 from the tilt, with the second engine contributing $8,886. Two mechanisms of comparable size, one of them not what I said it was.

The clamp binding rate you have been quoted is the wrong number. The 39.2% figure for 2026 is the widest single member’s rate, not the ensemble’s. The ensemble-level figure is 12.9%. Three definitions were circulating with none written down; it is pinned now, and the quoted one overstates the ensemble effect about threefold. Separately, the hypothesis that the clamp had collapsed ensemble diversity is rejected — effective diversity in 2026 sits mid-range and exposure is at its highest.

Deflation-adjusted view, and what is honestly claimed

Wave 19 spent zero alpha budget — a diagnostic consumes none — so both hypotheses remain available and the multiple-testing position is unchanged: deflated Sharpe 0.45–0.55 against a 0.90 bar, and a Harvey–Liu-adjusted Sharpe of 0.000 on the incumbent’s key comparison.

Ten consecutive alpha hypotheses have closed without a promotion, and the shift signal remains live. What this wave changes is the direction. The bad period is not a hostile market — by its own variables it was a mildly favourable one — it is not novel, and the incumbent is broken in it as badly as the challengers are. That combination points away from “find a mechanism that survives 2026” and toward the one testable question left: does the same failure appear in the named 2025 analog? Since no clean out-of-sample data exists anywhere, a prior occurrence of the same market state is the only quasi-out-of-sample check this program can construct. That is Wave 20.

E-1 also settles a question that had been left ambiguous: there is no clean historical out-of-sample window anywhere — not for the Solar baseline, and as of 2026-08-08 not for B-MOM or B1 either. The only genuinely virgin data is from 2026-08-01, it is nine days old, and it accrues at one quarter per quarter no matter how much research effort is applied to it. That is precisely why cross-instrument replication was worth running, and why its result carries weight.

Nothing in this program establishes that future profitability is achievable. The honest claim stays narrower: this process raises the probability of future success relative to a naively optimised system. The distribution remains extremely right-skewed — the top 1% of trades account for 160% of net profit, and removing the ten best days takes net from $198k to $72k.
