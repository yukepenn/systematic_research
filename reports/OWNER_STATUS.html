Two-thirds of the trading day produces one-fifth of the money, and one-quarter of it loses

This is the D4 diagnostic, and it is the most useful thing this wave produced. Incumbent P&L by time-of-day cohort over the full dev window:

Cohort (ET)Share of barsShare of P&LNetLocal vol ÷ threshold
EVENING 18:00–23:5926.0%−9.2%−$10,9890.61

The last column is why. The decision threshold is derived from a 460-bar volatility mean — and 460 three-minute bars is exactly one 23-hour session, so the threshold carries no time-of-day information at all while the volatility it is meant to normalise swings 11-fold across the day (0.372 at midnight, 4.105 at 09:33). That premise test was pre-registered with a falsification bar of 1.5×. It was not close.

M1 failed — and the reason is that the fix could not reach the place it was aimed at

Applying a session-mean-1 seasonal factor to the volatility estimate failed all three gates: Sharpe 0.558 vs 0.709, drawdown-tail $35,498 vs $27,162, and right-tail retention 80.5% against a 95% floor.

The cause is mechanical and I measured it rather than guessing. The threshold is resampled only when a trend flips, and flips cluster in exactly the loud parts of the day — so the factor in force at the moment that matters averages 1.54, not 1.00. The result is not a re-allocation of threshold across the day at all; it is a 64% widening of the threshold everywhere, which collapsed flips by 46% in every cohort including the overnight one it was supposed to tighten. My own spec asserted this was "a pure re-allocation." That assertion was wrong, and it was knowable in advance from the state machine's own semantics.

Which makes the null conditional, not final — and points somewhere specific. Every core challenge this program has ever run varied what the volatility estimate measures, what clock it is measured on, or where its clamp sits. None has ever varied when the threshold is sampled. That is the ranked lead into Wave 19.

M5 killed the "we just lacked statistical power" excuse for the ATR blend

Wave 14 closed the ATR-blend candidate having failed one prong of one gate — the drawdown-tail probability at 0.753 against a 0.85 bar — and blamed 4.4 years of daily data. This wave rebuilt the identical construction, weight frozen, on ES, RTY and YM over the same era, and pooled the evidence with a bootstrap that resamples the same dates across all four instruments so the dependence is preserved exactly.

The Sharpe effect replicated: pooled probability 0.911. The tail effect did not: pooled 0.784. Roughly four times the effective evidence — the instruments' mechanism increments turn out to be nearly independent (cross-correlation 0.029, versus 0.677 for their raw P&L, giving an effective sample size of 3.68 of 4) — moved that prong by 0.031 and it still did not clear the bar. The power hypothesis has now been tested and rejected. Verdict PARTIAL, which was pre-registered as not a pass. The lead stays closed, now on two independent grounds.

One thing not to misread: ES, RTY and YM all lose money under this construction (Sharpe −0.045, −0.633, −0.563). The system does not travel; only the paired increment does. They are replication samples and nothing here proposes trading them.

And a correction to what I told you yesterday about warmup

I attributed the start-state gap mainly to the 460-bar volatility window. Measured, that is wrong: the Solar core re-synchronises from a cold start in 2 to 4 sessions, because it is a self-synchronising state machine and its volatility estimate uses an expanding mean rather than going undefined. What actually binds is the higher-timeframe tilt at 51 sessions — a 50-session moving average, lagged one session, which simply does not exist before then. So a fresh run started 2026-01-01 has no tilt at all for roughly half the January–May window. The conclusion you were given — that a sliced year and a fresh-start year are different objects — stands, and is stronger than the reason I gave for it.
