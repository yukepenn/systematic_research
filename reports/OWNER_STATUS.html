Two-thirds of the trading day produces one-fifth of the money, and one-quarter of it loses

This is the D4 diagnostic, and it is the most useful thing this wave produced. Incumbent P&L by time-of-day cohort over the full dev window:

Cohort (ET)Share of barsShare of P&LNetLocal vol ÷ threshold
EVENING 18:00–23:5926.0%−9.2%−$10,9890.61

The last column is why. The decision threshold is derived from a 460-bar volatility mean — and 460 three-minute bars is exactly one 23-hour session, so the threshold carries no time-of-day information at all while the volatility it is meant to normalise swings 11-fold across the day (0.372 at midnight, 4.105 at 09:33). That premise test was pre-registered with a falsification bar of 1.5×. It was not close.

M1 failed, and the review closed the door I had left open

Applying a session-mean-1 seasonal factor to the volatility estimate failed all three gates: Sharpe 0.558 vs 0.709, drawdown-tail $35,498 vs $27,162, right-tail retention 80.5% against a 95% floor.

The cause is mechanical and I measured it. The threshold is resampled only when a trend flips, and flips cluster in the loud parts of the day — so the factor in force at the moment that matters averages 1.54, not 1.00. The result was not a re-allocation of threshold across the day; it was a 39–64% widening of the threshold everywhere. My own spec asserted it was "a pure re-allocation." Wrong, and knowable in advance from the state machine's semantics.

I then called the null "conditional" and queued two de-confounded constructions for Wave 19. The reviewer ran both — they take eight seconds each — and both are worse. Exposure-neutral: Sharpe 0.411, tail $40,759. Per-bar resampling in both arms, which is the clean test: ΔSharpe −0.303, probability of improvement 0.116. The axis closes unconditionally and the Wave-19 lead I proposed is withdrawn. Two further retractions came with it: this was in effect an exposure change (a third less exposure for a quarter less profit — net per unit of exposure barely moved), and the failure is not "wide-margin" at all — ΔSharpe −0.150 sits in [−0.64, +0.33], and three-quarters of the damage is in the last 106 sessions, with M1 actually beating the incumbent in 2024 and 2025.

M5: the ATR blend does not replicate — and my first write-up of it overstated the result

Wave 14 closed this candidate having failed one prong of one gate — drawdown-tail probability 0.753 against a 0.85 bar — and blamed a shortage of data. This wave rebuilt the identical construction, weight frozen, on ES, RTY and YM over the same era, pooling with a bootstrap that resamples the same dates across all four instruments so their dependence is preserved exactly.

I reported that the Sharpe effect replicated (0.911) while the tail effect did not (0.784). The reviewer showed the passing prong was carried by NQ — the instrument that generated the hypothesis in the first place. On the three instruments that did not generate it, the prongs are 0.8223 and 0.7108 — both below the bar — and not one of them clears 0.85 on either prong individually. The corrected statement is that neither effect replicates, which is a stronger closure than the one I wrote. Verdict PARTIAL stands, and PARTIAL was pre-registered as not a pass.

Two things not to misread. ES, RTY and YM all lose money under this construction (Sharpe −0.045, −0.633, −0.563) — the system does not travel, only the paired increment was ever under test, and nothing proposes trading them. And the reviewer tested eight alternative ways of pooling: none flips the verdict, and the only rule that would pass is post-hoc deletion of the instrument that failed — which is exactly what pre-registration exists to prevent.

Both mechanism results are carried by the same 9% of the sample

Independent runs, different substrates, same 106 sessions. M1's failure is 74.5% concentrated in 2026-01-02 → 2026-05-29; dropping those same sessions moves M5's pooled probabilities from 0.911 / 0.784 to 0.766 / 0.655. On yearly sign agreement every instrument in M5 — including NQ — is only 3 of 5, below the 4-of-5 bar this program applied to a different candidate one wave earlier.

Neither spec pre-registered a chronology check, and that is a design defect in both. It is now a standing rule that future mechanism specs must carry one alongside the primary statistic. This also means the two failures this wave are less about the mechanisms than they look, and more about a recent regime that neither construction handles — which is itself the more interesting question.

And a correction to what I told you yesterday about warmup

I attributed the start-state gap mainly to the 460-bar volatility window. Measured, that is wrong: the Solar core re-synchronises from a cold start in 2 to 4 sessions, because it is a self-synchronising state machine and its volatility estimate uses an expanding mean rather than going undefined. What actually binds is the higher-timeframe tilt at 51 sessions — a 50-session moving average, lagged one session, which simply does not exist before then. So a fresh run started 2026-01-01 has no tilt at all for roughly half the January–May window. The conclusion you were given — that a sliced year and a fresh-start year are different objects — stands, and is stronger than the reason I gave for it.

Deflation-adjusted view, and what is honestly claimed

Wave 18 spent both permitted alpha hypotheses and neither produced a candidate, so the multiple-testing position is unchanged and the trial count rose: deflated Sharpe 0.45–0.55 against a 0.90 bar, and a Harvey–Liu-adjusted Sharpe of 0.000 on the incumbent's key comparison.

Ten consecutive alpha hypotheses have now closed without a promotion. Under the standing rules that is a signal to change what is being searched rather than to search harder in the same space. I proposed a direction for Wave 19 — the strategy core's threshold-sampling rule — and then the review tested it and it was worse, so I am withdrawing it rather than carrying a lead the evidence has already killed. What actually survives as a pointer is narrower and more honest: the day's P&L is concentrated in regular trading hours while a quarter of the clock loses money, and both of this wave's failures are concentrated in the same recent 106 sessions. Those are the two things worth aiming at next, and neither is a threshold re-weighting.

E-1 also settles a question that had been left ambiguous: there is no clean historical out-of-sample window anywhere — not for the Solar baseline, and as of 2026-08-08 not for B-MOM or B1 either. The only genuinely virgin data is from 2026-08-01, it is nine days old, and it accrues at one quarter per quarter no matter how much research effort is applied to it. That is precisely why cross-instrument replication was worth running, and why its result carries weight.

Nothing in this program establishes that future profitability is achievable. The honest claim stays narrower: this process raises the probability of future success relative to a naively optimised system. The distribution remains extremely right-skewed — the top 1% of trades account for 160% of net profit, and removing the ten best days takes net from $198k to $72k.
