## Source-quality assessment

Evidence base is three-tiered. (Tier 1, authoritative-by-construction) NinjaTrader's official NT8 help guide pages document the simulator's actual code paths: "Understanding Historical Fill Processing" (https://ninjatrader.com/support/helpguides/nt8/understanding_historical_fill_.htm), "Discrepancies: Real-Time vs Backtest" (https://ninjatrader.com/support/helpguides/nt8/discrepancies_real-time_vs_bac.htm), and "Historical Order Backfill Logic" (https://ninjatrader.com/support/helpguides/nt8/historical_order_backfill_logic.htm). These are vendor documentation, not peer-reviewed, but they are definitionally correct about what the simulator does (they describe its implementation); they say nothing about how markets behave. (Tier 2, semi-authoritative) NinjaTrader staff replies on forum.ninjatrader.com (threads 1133897 market orders in backtest fill at next bar open; 1203697/1124390 ExitOnSessionCloseSeconds is real-time-only and historical exits occur at the close of the last session bar; 1247243 "Tick Replay was NOT designed to provide accuracy in backtesting concerning order fills"; 1190815/1251670 merge offsets downloaded from NT server or computed locally). Staff statements are consistent across threads and with the help guide (informal replication), but are version-dependent and the forum has since migrated (old URLs now redirect/404), so I preserved thread IDs. (Tier 3, practitioner lore) e.g., the Aeromir analysis (https://futures.aeromir.com/post/111/ninjatrader-backtesting-explained-which-method-should-you-trust) argues Analyzer results are "functionally identical" to live for OnBarClose market-order strategies with no intrabar orders — a reasoned claim with zero empirical measurement; treat as hypothesis, not fact. (Peer-reviewed anchor) The one economically load-bearing external mechanism — backtests on last-trade prices omit the effective spread paid by real market orders — is solidly peer-reviewed: Roll (1984, Journal of Finance, implicit effective spread) and Blume & Stambaugh (1983, JFE, bid-ask bounce bias in trade-price returns); NQ tick/contract specs from CME (https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/equities/nasdaq/e-mini-nasdaq-100.html); e-mini depth/spread evidence in the floor-vs-e-mini liquidity literature (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265449645). No peer-reviewed study of NT8 simulator fidelity exists anywhere; magnitude claims (e.g., "Standard ≈ High for market-order strategies") have never been formally replicated and must be measured in-house — which is what the hypotheses below do.

## Key mechanisms

- Standard fill resolution is a deterministic path model, not tick data: each historical bar is decomposed into three virtual bars — Open→nearer extreme, extreme→other extreme, extreme→Close (Open→High→Low→Close if Open is closer to High, else Open→Low→High→Close) — and all order fills are simulated against that synthetic path (NT8 help: understanding_historical_fill_.htm). This path assumption only binds for orders that trigger INTRABAR (stops/limits/targets); it is irrelevant for orders placed at bar boundaries.
- Market orders submitted from an OnBarClose calculation fill at the OPEN of the next bar in historical processing, clamped inside the bar (help: historical_order_backfill_logic.htm; staff confirmation, forum thread 1133897). Critically, live OnBarClose behavior is structurally the same (submit after close tick, fill at next available price), so for a close-basis market-order strategy the backtest's TIMING model is faithful — the divergence from reality is almost entirely PRICE (spread/latency), not timing.
- The fill price used is a last-trade price (bar Open), not the ask/bid a real market order would cross. Peer-reviewed microstructure (Roll 1984 JF; Blume & Stambaugh 1983 JFE) establishes that trade-price-based simulated fills omit the effective half-spread per side. NQ minimum tick = 0.25 pt = $5 (CME specs), typical inside spread 1 tick RTH and 1–2 ticks overnight, so with slippage=0 the canonical baseline embeds exactly zero microstructure cost: an optimistic bias of roughly $5–$15 per round-trip, i.e. ~$15k–$44k across 2,915 trades against $146,440.60 net — the single largest realism bias in our setup.
- Slippage in the Analyzer is applied only to market, stop-market, and market-if-touched orders (never limits), and is CAPPED by the next bar's high/low: 'you cannot have more slippage than the high/low price of the next bar' (help: understanding_historical_fill_.htm). Consequence: configured slippage systematically UNDER-applies in narrow bars, so a 1-tick slippage run is a lower bound on the intended haircut — the applied-vs-configured gap must be audited in Python, not assumed.
- Exit-on-session-close is simulated as a fill at the CLOSE price of the last bar of the session; ExitOnSessionCloseSeconds is a real-time-only property with no historical effect (staff, threads 1203697 and 1124390). Live, the flat-by-close exit fires ~30s before 17:00 ET into thin liquidity and crosses a wider spread. Since our SAR strategy exits at every session close (~520+ exits over the window), this is a systematic optimistic bias concentrated at the worst-liquidity minute of the day.
- Without Tick Replay, historical processing is ALWAYS effectively Calculate.OnBarClose regardless of the Calculate setting ('during backtest, strategies can ONLY be processed at the close of each bar' — discrepancies_real-time_vs_bac.htm); Tick Replay replays only Last events (best inside bid/ask stored only as-of each trade), cannot replay bid/ask event streams, is incompatible with High order fill resolution, and staff state it 'was NOT designed to provide accuracy in backtesting concerning order fills' (thread 1247243). For an OnBarClose strategy Tick Replay adds nothing.
- MergeBackAdjusted stitches expiry months at NT-defined rollover dates using an ADDITIVE offset (downloaded from NT's data server, or computed locally if absent — threads 1190815/1251670). Additive shifts preserve point differences (so difference/momentum-based indicator math and per-trade point PnL are invariant) but distort ratios, percentages, and absolute price levels; offsets can differ across machines/downloads, which is a reproducibility hazard for any level-dependent computation. Backtests never simulate rolls or roll costs — immaterial here because the strategy is flat at every session close and no trade spans a roll (per campaign parity work).
- High Order Fill Resolution replaces the virtual-bar path with a secondary 1-tick series for fill determination only. For a strategy with zero intrabar orders, Standard vs High should differ only through (a) session-close fills and (b) tick-vs-minute data inconsistencies at the provider level ('single tick differences can generate completely different looking charts' — discrepancies page), making the Standard-vs-High comparison a cheap, near-zero-DOF integrity audit of our entire simulation stack.

## What does NOT transfer to 1-min NQ

- Same-bar stop/target ambiguity horror stories (the most-documented NT8 backtest pitfall, and the main reason High OFR exists): our baseline SolarWaveRKReplicaV0 uses only close-basis market reversals and session-close exits — no intrabar stop-loss or profit-target orders — so the OHLC path-guessing problem largely does not bind. It becomes binding the moment any frontier variant adds intrabar stops/targets/trailing exits; at that point High OFR (1-tick) must become mandatory, not optional.
- Tick Replay as a realism upgrade: it changes when INDICATORS calculate (historical OnEachTick), not how orders fill, is Last-events-only, and is incompatible with High OFR. For an OnBarClose close-basis strategy it changes nothing and burns large compute; forum advice recommending it is answering a different problem.
- NT8's Renko/HeikenAshi backtest warnings ('standard Renko cannot accurately simulate reversals; fake open prices'): despite the RenkoKings vendor branding, our canonical runs are on 1-minute time bars of Last, so these warnings do not apply — but they become fatal if anyone proposes validating against actual Renko bars in the Analyzer.
- ExitOnSessionCloseSeconds tuning: it is a real-time-only property, so the live exit-timing question cannot be studied inside the Strategy Analyzer at all; it must be approximated analytically in Python (H3) or measured later in sim/live parity runs.
- Institutional market-impact models (Almgren-Chriss style) and the AQR-scale trading-cost literature: at DefaultQuantity=1 on NQ (thousands of contracts resting at inside levels), permanent/temporary impact is negligible; the transferable cost components are spread + latency only. Do not import equity or large-order impact magnitudes.
- Blume-Stambaugh/Roll magnitude estimates from cash equities (spreads of tens of bps on low-price stocks): the MECHANISM (trade-price fills omit effective spread) transfers; the MAGNITUDES do not — NQ effective spread is ~1 tick = ~0.9bp of notional. Use futures-specific numbers ($5/tick) only.
- Rollover execution costs: MergeBackAdjusted backtests never roll, but since the strategy is flat at every session close, live trading would also carry no position across rolls — zero real-world roll cost, so the simulator's omission is coincidentally exact for this campaign (would NOT hold for any always-in variant that holds across 17:00–18:00).

## Hypotheses

### DR07-H1 — For the frozen canonical config (SolarWaveRKReplicaV0, Type 1, 90/179/5/10/true/10, canonical window), switching Order Fill Resolution from Standard to High (1-tick secondary series) leaves the trade list essentially unchanged: >=99% of trades have identical entry/exit bar timestamps, and |net profit delta| <= 2% of $146,440.60.

**Mechanism.** NT8's virtual-bar path assumption (Open→extreme→extreme→Close) only determines fills for orders triggered intrabar; market orders from OnBarClose logic fill at the next bar's open under BOTH resolutions (historical_order_backfill_logic.htm; forum 1133897). A strategy with zero intrabar orders should therefore be fill-resolution-invariant up to tick-vs-minute data-feed inconsistencies. If it is not invariant, either hidden intrabar order paths exist in the replica or the provider's tick and minute histories disagree materially — both of which would poison every downstream experiment run at Standard resolution.

**Experiment.** One Strategy Analyzer run: canonical config, canonical window, all frozen-truth settings identical except Order Fill Resolution = High with 1-tick granularity (slippage 0). Ingest via runlib.py into a new immutable runs/<run_id>/; Python: outer-join trades.parquet against the canonical run on entry timestamp; report (a) fraction of matched entry/exit timestamps, (b) distribution of fill-price deltas in ticks, (c) net/PF/DD deltas, (d) any trades present in one run only.

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: >=99% of 2,915 trades match on entry AND exit timestamps, median |fill delta| = 0 ticks, 95th percentile <= 1 tick, |net delta| <= $2,929 (2%). FAIL: any threshold violated → freeze new experiments until the divergent trades are root-caused (hidden intrabar orders vs data inconsistency); if intrabar orders are found, High OFR becomes mandatory campaign-wide. Criteria fixed before the run.

**Priority:** high · **Degrees of freedom:** 0 tuned parameters — single documented toggle on a frozen config; thresholds pre-registered. · **Overfitting risk:** None — pure validation/integrity audit; no selection is performed on the outcome, and neither outcome changes strategy parameters.

### DR07-H2 — The canonical edge survives realistic microstructure frictions: with 1 tick/side slippage (the documented NQ RTH inside spread) the strategy retains net > $0 and PF >= 1.05 over the canonical window; the analytically predicted haircut is ~$29,150 (2,915 trades x 2 sides x $5), implying net ≈ $117k and PF ≈ 1.10 if slippage applies fully.

**Mechanism.** Backtest fills at last-trade prices with slippage=0 embed zero spread/latency cost (Roll 1984; Blume & Stambaugh 1983 — trade-price fills omit the effective half-spread); real NQ market orders cross a 1-tick RTH / 1-2-tick overnight spread ($5/tick, CME specs) plus latency drift that is adverse on average because SAR reversals fire during momentum bursts. NT8's slippage setting models this but is capped by the next bar's range (understanding_historical_fill_.htm), so applied slippage may undershoot configured slippage.

**Experiment.** Two Analyzer runs (canonical config/window, Standard OFR): slippage = 1 tick and slippage = 2 ticks. In Python, independently recompute the haircut from the zero-slippage trades.parquet (subtract k x $5 per side, k ∈ {1,2}) and compare to the NT runs; the NT-minus-analytic gap directly measures how often the bar-range cap truncated slippage (report truncation frequency and dollar shortfall). Log all three friction levels (0/1/2 ticks) into the registry as standing columns for the leaderboard.

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: at 1 tick/side, net > $0 AND PF >= 1.05. STRONG PASS: at 2 ticks/side, net > $0 AND PF >= 1.05 (analytic prediction ≈ $88k net, PF ≈ 1.08 — so strong-pass is genuinely at risk, not guaranteed). FAIL: net <= $0 at 1 tick/side → the edge is thinner than unavoidable frictions; the whole line is demoted pending friction-aware redesign. Also pre-registered: if NT-applied slippage < 85% of configured aggregate slippage (cap truncation), the analytic Python haircut becomes the campaign's authoritative friction number, not the NT run.

**Priority:** high · **Degrees of freedom:** ~0 — slippage levels {1,2} ticks are fixed ex-ante from documented spread data, not searched. · **Overfitting risk:** Low, with one standing hazard: never select future variants by which slippage assumption flatters them. Mitigation is pre-registered: 1 tick/side becomes the default reporting friction for all leaderboard entries if H2 passes.

### DR07-H3 — Session-close exit fills are an immaterial optimistic bias: repricing every session-close exit to the close of the PRIOR 1-min bar (proxy for the real-time exit ~30-60s before 17:00 ET) plus a 2-tick penalty per exit changes canonical net profit by less than $7,500 (~5%).

**Mechanism.** Historically NT8 fills the session-close exit at the Close of the last session bar with zero spread (forum 1203697); ExitOnSessionCloseSeconds is real-time-only (forum 1124390), so live exits occur earlier, at a different price, crossing a spread that is widest at 16:59-17:00 ET. With ~520+ daily flat-at-close exits in the window, even a small per-exit bias compounds; but if the strategy's daily close positions are small-PnL-at-stake at that minute, the aggregate effect may be negligible.

**Experiment.** Pure Python on the canonical run's trades.parquet + daily_equity.parquet (no new NT run): identify all exits whose exit time equals the last bar of each 18:00→17:00 ET session; recompute trade PnL substituting the prior bar's close (available from the archived 1-min payload) and subtracting 2 ticks x $5; recompute net, PF, max DD, and per-month deltas.

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: |total net delta| < $7,500 AND no calendar month's PnL flips sign. FAIL: either violated → session-close realism becomes a required adjustment in all reported metrics, and any live-intent variant must implement an explicit time-based exit (e.g., 16:58 ET) instead of relying on IsExitOnSessionCloseStrategy. Thresholds fixed before computation.

**Priority:** medium · **Degrees of freedom:** 1 — the choice of proxy price (prior-bar close) is a single pre-committed convention; the 2-tick penalty is fixed from spread evidence. · **Overfitting risk:** Very low — post-hoc accounting decomposition of an existing frozen run; no strategy parameter is touched.

### DR07-H4 — The recovered Solar Wave signal is invariant to additive back-adjustment: adding a constant offset K to the entire price series (simulating a different MergeBackAdjusted offset) produces bit-identical signal timestamps, and therefore the canonical result does not depend on NT's downloadable/locally-computed merge offsets.

**Mechanism.** MergeBackAdjusted applies additive per-contract offsets stitched at rollover dates (forum 1190815); offsets may be re-downloaded or recomputed and are not guaranteed stable across machines/time (forum 1251670). Difference-based indicator math is invariant to additive shifts; ratio-, percentage-, or absolute-level-based math is not. The campaign has fully reverse-engineered the vendor math (solarwave-recovered-math), so invariance is directly checkable — if it fails, the frozen baseline is only reproducible with the exact frozen dataset, and any level-dependent term is also a live-vs-backtest divergence (live sees unadjusted prices).

**Experiment.** Pure Python: run the recovered Solar Wave computation (90/179/5/10/true/10) on the archived canonical 1-min series and on the same series shifted by K ∈ {+500, -500, +5000} points; diff the full signal/direction time series. Optionally corroborate with one Analyzer run on MergeNonBackAdjusted over a single-contract sub-window (e.g., NQ 09-26 native segment) compared trade-for-trade against the canonical run's same segment.

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: 100% identical signal transition timestamps for all K (exact integer/bool equality, no tolerance). FAIL: any divergence → (a) document the level-dependent term in the registry, (b) quantify PnL sensitivity per 100-point offset, (c) flag the frozen dataset as the sole source of truth and add an offset-stability audit to every future data refresh. Criteria fixed ex-ante.

**Priority:** medium · **Degrees of freedom:** 0 — K values arbitrary by construction (invariance must hold for all K); no tuning. · **Overfitting risk:** None — mathematical invariance test on frozen artifacts.

### DR07-H5 — The edge is not concentrated in the first post-signal minute: delaying every entry/reversal fill by one additional 1-min bar (fill at open of bar t+2 instead of t+1) retains net >= 50% of canonical net ($73,220) and PF >= 1.07.

**Mechanism.** The backtest's only timing assumption is fill-at-next-open with zero latency. SAR signals on a momentum system cluster in fast bars where real fills suffer adverse drift beyond the next-open print; a one-bar delay stress upper-bounds ALL sub-minute latency/queue/spread-widening effects simultaneously. If PnL collapses under a one-bar delay, the paper edge lives in the first ~60 seconds after signal — exactly the region where Standard minute-bar simulation is least trustworthy — and live capture risk is severe. (Mechanism is inference from momentum-decay logic plus the documented fill-timing model; no external study of this specific strategy class exists.)

**Experiment.** New strategy class SolarWaveRKReplicaV0_DelayN_v1 (renamed per hot-reload convention): identical signal computation, but execution deferred by one bar (signal latch at close of t, submit market order at close of t+1, filling at open of t+2). One Analyzer run, canonical window/settings, Standard OFR, slippage 0, ingested via runlib.py. Python: compare net, PF, DD, per-trade PnL distribution vs canonical; also report the implied per-trade cost of one minute of delay as a latency-sensitivity coefficient ($/trade/minute).

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: net >= $73,220 AND PF >= 1.07. MARGINAL (flag, don't kill): net in [$36,610, $73,220) or PF in [1.03, 1.07). FAIL: net < $36,610 (25%) or PF < 1.03 → classify the baseline as execution-timing-fragile; all realism reporting must then use H2's 2-tick friction tier, and live-intent claims are blocked pending sim/live parity measurement. Thresholds fixed before the run.

**Priority:** medium · **Degrees of freedom:** 1 — delay length fixed at exactly one bar ex-ante; no scanning over delays permitted (scanning would convert this probe into a tuning axis). · **Overfitting risk:** Low if used only as a pass/fail robustness gate. Standing caution registered: the delayed variant must never be promoted as a strategy candidate on the basis of this run — it exists only to measure timing sensitivity.

## Decision

Decision: admit DR07-H2 (friction fragility, 1-2 tick slippage) and DR07-H1 (Standard-vs-High invariance audit) to the active frontier immediately — both are ~zero-DOF, one-to-two Analyzer runs each, and they gate the validity of the entire campaign: H2 answers whether the $146k canonical edge survives the single largest documented optimistic bias (zero spread/latency cost, predicted haircut ~$29k at 1 tick/side, ~$58k at 2 ticks), and H1 verifies the structural claim that a close-basis market-order SAR strategy is immune to NT8's virtual-bar path assumption, which is the precondition for trusting every cheap Standard-resolution run we will ever do. Admit DR07-H4 (back-adjustment invariance) next — it is a free Python-only test that protects reproducibility of the frozen baseline against NT's mutable merge offsets and simultaneously certifies live-vs-backtest signal equivalence across the roll boundary. DR07-H3 (session-close exit repricing) and DR07-H5 (one-bar delay stress) enter as medium-priority robustness probes: H3 is a free post-hoc decomposition of an already-frozen run; H5 costs one new versioned strategy class and quantifies whether the edge survives outside the first post-signal minute, the region where minute-bar simulation is least trustworthy. Overall verdict on the key question: for this specific configuration (OnBarClose, market orders only, flat at session close, 1 contract), NT8 Standard minute-bar resolution has faithful TIMING semantics (fill-at-next-open matches live OnBarClose structure) and its notorious intrabar path-guessing pitfall does not bind; the biases that remain are almost entirely OPTIMISTIC and price-side — omitted effective spread on every execution, zero latency drift, spread-free session-close exits at the day's thinnest minute, and the slippage-cap rule that silently truncates even configured friction in narrow bars — with essentially no conservative bias offsetting them. The campaign's realism policy should therefore change on H2 completion: all leaderboard metrics get a standing 1-tick/side friction column, with 0-tick numbers retained only for UI/MCP parity against the frozen truth.
