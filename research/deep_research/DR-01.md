## Key mechanisms

- SAR P&L identity in DC coordinates: decompose the close path into alternating δ-trends (δ = 179 ticks for our Type-1 flip rule; RE01 proved TrendMultiplier is inert). Each segment = DC part (δ) + overshoot ω; a stop-and-reverse position entered at DC confirmation and exited at the next flip earns exactly ω − δ per segment (minus close-crossing excess and costs). [Inference — elementary algebra on the recovered RE01 model; segment definitions per Glattfelder et al 2011, https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1040]
- The overshoot scaling law IS the no-alpha null: for a driftless diffusion the maximum-before-drawdown-δ is exponentially distributed with mean δ (Lehoczky 1977; Zhang & Hadjiliadis 2009, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304414909000155), hence E[ω] = δ and E[SAR P&L] = 0 pre-cost, strictly negative after costs. Glattfelder et al's empirical 'mean overshoot ≈ threshold' in FX therefore confirms near-martingale behavior, not a tradable pattern. Our entire full-history edge ($259k/10,182 trades ≈ 6 ticks gross per trade on δ=179) is a ~3% upward deviation of the overshoot ratio r = E[ω]/δ from its martingale value 1.0 [inference — to be verified by the DC01 decomposition]. This makes the thinness of the edge a mathematical statement, not a mood.
- Event-rate/volatility relation: number of DC events scales as N(δ) ∝ (σ/δ)² (scaling law 5–6 of Glattfelder et al 2011; inverted as the DC volatility estimator σ² ≈ δ²·N/T by Petrov, Golub, Olsen 2019, https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/12/2/54). A fixed-tick δ therefore has trade frequency proportional to σ² and selectivity proportional to 1/σ — this is the exact mechanism behind the registered H-006 finding that 179 ticks fell from 17.8 to 10.4 per-bar-vol units (−41%) from 2023 to 2025.
- Cost-optimal threshold theory (the answer to 'is there theory for optimal δ vs volatility'): no explicit theory exists in the literature, but one follows from combining the two laws above with fixed per-flip cost c: expected profit per unit time Π(δ) ∝ σ²[(r(δ)−1)/δ − c/δ²], maximized at δ* = 2c/(r−1) — which is INDEPENDENT of σ if the overshoot ratio r is scale-invariant in tick space, but proportional to σ if r is invariant in δ/σ space. [Inference — derived here from cited laws.] Which invariance holds on NQ closes is an empirical question that decides whether H-006 (vol-scaled threshold) can work, and it is measurable WITHOUT burning any NT8 configs.
- Overshoot memorylessness: exponential ω implies a flat hazard of trend termination — given the trend has already extended x beyond the DC point, the expected further extension is still δ, regardless of x. Under the null there is NO information in 'how far the trend has run' for exit timing; any empirical hazard slope (decreasing = fat tails = hold runners, increasing = exhaustion = giveback locks can work) is precisely the quantity that should arbitrate the thesis §7 exit debate and the registered H-007 split reverse/exit distances. [Inference grounded in Zhang & Hadjiliadis 2009.]
- Multi-threshold intrinsic networks: the Alpha Engine coordinates DC dissections at multiple thresholds ({δ, 2δ, 4δ}) as a multi-scale state description (Golub, Glattfelder, Olsen 2017, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2951348); Tsang's TMV/T/R indicators profile regimes the same way (https://kar.kent.ac.uk/99237/1/s10462-022-10307-0.pdf). Deviations of the small-δ/large-δ event-rate ratio from the BM-predicted (δ_large/δ_small)² are a scale-break (roughness) statistic — a DC-native candidate to formalize SW01's empirical finding that high-vol, high-flip regimes carry the P&L.
- Event-time sampling: DC segments (like volume buckets, Easley/López de Prado/O'Hara 2012, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2034858) devolatilize the series — intrinsic time ticks faster in volatile regimes (Guillaume et al 1997, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s007800050018). Practical import for us: DC segments/episodes are the natural blocking unit for bootstrap and CPCV (the thesis' trend-episode bootstrap gets its theoretical justification here), and per-segment statistics are the correct sufficient statistics for the S1 spatial sweep's entire curve.
- Close-basis discretization: evaluating the δ-trigger on 1-min closes rather than the continuous path adds a crossing excess ε = |close − theoretical trigger| ≥ 0 at every flip, i.e., an effective threshold δ_eff = δ + E[ε|state] and an effective per-flip execution drag that both scale with per-bar volatility. [Inference — no literature found on DC-on-sampled-bars; RE01's 'single-point path noise' observation is the same phenomenon.] This state-dependent wedge matters for comparing tick-space vs vol-space threshold theories and is measurable exactly from the SW01 bar ledger.

## What does NOT transfer to 1-min NQ

- The specific FX scaling-law constants and exponents (Glattfelder et al 2011): estimated on 1999–2009 bid-ask midpoint TICK data of 13 currency pairs in a decentralized 24h market. Our data is 1-min last-price closes of an exchange-traded index future with a session close and a daily maintenance halt. Only the functional forms plausibly transfer; every coefficient must be re-estimated, and any δ below ~3–5 per-bar sigmas (≈45–90 ticks in 2025 vol) is in a regime where close-sampling destroys the laws entirely.
- The Alpha Engine's profitability (Sharpe ~3 claim): it is a COUNTER-trend liquidity-provision scheme that scales into positions against overshoots with tiny FX thresholds, effectively earning spread and mean-reversion at near-zero cost. It is unreviewed, its cost model is optimistic, and its economics are the opposite of our directional-persistence family; at 1-contract NQ with $4.36 RT plus slippage, coastline market-making is infeasible. At most its overshoot-exhaustion idea informs the future Family B (failed-breakout) design.
- DC + genetic-programming/ML profitability results (Kampouridis, Otero, Adegboye, Bakhach, Rayment et al): FX pairs, frequently 10-min or daily sampled physical-time data, rules evolved across hundreds of datasets with in-sample selection and no Harvey-Liu-grade multiple-testing control; claimed outperformance vs buy-and-hold is not a meaningful benchmark for a futures SAR system. Their indicator definitions (TMV, OSV, T, R) transfer; their profit claims do not.
- Published 'good' DC thresholds (0.1%–1% in FX studies; the 0.5% Alexander filter literature): threshold economics are set by the instrument's cost-to-volatility ratio and our close-basis trigger; NQ 2022–2026 values must come from our own r(δ) measurement, not from any paper.
- Fama-Blume's death-by-costs verdict on filter rules transfers only as a base-rate warning, not quantitatively: their costs were 1960s stock round-trips (~1%), ours are ~1.3 ticks equivalent on a 179-tick threshold. The shape of the conclusion (small thresholds die first) survives; the location of the profitability frontier does not.
- FX/BTC weekly seasonality patterns of DC activity (Petrov et al 2019): CME index futures have a different session topology (18:00 ET reopen, 16:30–17:00 close effects already seen in SW02a); seasonality must be re-profiled on our ledger if ever used.
- Volume-clock sampling as implemented in the literature (Easley/LdP/O'Hara): requires tick or per-bar volume aggregation and would change NT8 bar semantics entirely (different state machine, per thesis §6.1); event-time enters our campaign as an analysis/blocking tool, not as a data type change in Wave 1.
- The 1−1/e renewal constant (arXiv 2511.14408): single unreviewed preprint, assets unspecified; treat as a curiosity to replicate for free inside DC01, never as a design input.

## Hypotheses

### DR01-H1 (DC01_OVERSHOOT_DECOMPOSITION) — On NQ 1-min closes 2022-01→2026-07, the mean overshoot ratio r = E[ω]/δ at δ=179 ticks exceeds the martingale null value 1.0 by an amount whose per-segment dollar equivalent covers commission + 1-tick slippage, and the per-segment identity P&L(ω − δ − crossing excess − costs, with session-close truncation terms) reconciles to the NT8 slip-0 trade ledger within 2% — i.e., the baseline's edge IS excess overshoot, not an accounting artifact. Additionally, the discrete hazard of trend termination is non-flat in overshoot depth (rejecting exponential memorylessness).

**Mechanism.** For any driftless diffusion the maximum-before-drawdown-δ is Exp(mean δ) (Lehoczky 1977; Zhang & Hadjiliadis 2009), so E[ω]=δ and SAR earns exactly zero pre-cost; the Glattfelder overshoot scaling law confirms this null holds to ~±5% in FX. Any SAR profit must therefore appear as r>1 (trend persistence at that scale) and is fully decomposable per segment. The hazard shape tests memorylessness: decreasing hazard = fat overshoot tails = keep runners / no profit caps; increasing hazard = exhaustion = informs H-007 exit-distance splitting.

**Experiment.** Pure Python on the archived full-history close ledger (src/analytics/solarwave.py code path; immutable run dir with spec.yaml committed before results). (1) DC dissection at δ ∈ {45, 90, 135, 179, 225, 270, 358} ticks; per-segment record: direction, DC/OS lengths and durations, crossing excess ε at both ends, session-close truncation flag, year, vol tercile (reuse SW01 tags). (2) At δ=179: reconcile Σ per-segment identity P&L (incl. the 46%-untaken-signal accounting) against the NT8 slip-0 ledger. (3) Estimate r(δ) with segment-block bootstrap CIs, per year and per vol tercile. (4) Discrete hazard h(x) = P(flip next bar | overshoot ≥ x) in x/δ buckets {0–0.25, 0.25–0.5, 0.5–1, 1–2, >2}. (5) Free rider: DC-to-total-event ratio vs the 1−1/e preprint. Optional preregistered replication read on ES 1-min closes (same code, same grid) — deviation signs should match NQ if the mechanism is index-futures-wide.

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS (edge-is-overshoot confirmed): 95% segment-block bootstrap CI for mean(ω)−δ at δ=179 excludes 0 with positive sign on full history; r>1 in ≥4 of 5 calendar years; ledger reconciliation gap ≤2% of gross (or fully explained by enumerated truncation/skip terms). FAIL: CI includes 0 → the trend-persistence story is dead and the edge must be re-attributed to timing/session effects (consistent with SW01b); any reconciliation gap >2% unexplained → integrity stop. Hazard read (preregistered secondary): monotone trend test across buckets at p<0.05 decides 'fat-tail' vs 'exhaustion'; flat hazard = no exit-timing information exists and all profit-target/giveback proposals in thesis §7 are rejected without further testing.

**Priority:** high · **Degrees of freedom:** 0 tuned. δ grid, bucket edges, block length, and all read rules preregistered; nothing is selected by results. One analysis choice (segment-block vs day-block bootstrap) fixed in spec before run. · **Overfitting risk:** Very low — measurement/attribution with preregistered reads on already-consumed research data; consumes no fresh data and no NT8 candidate configs. Main risk is interpretive (post-hoc storytelling from the per-year breakdown), mitigated by writing all reads into spec.yaml first.

### DR01-H2 (DC02_THRESHOLD_INVARIANCE) — The overshoot-ratio curve r(δ) on NQ closes is more stable across years/vol regimes when δ is expressed in volatility units (δ/σ) than in fixed ticks: the inter-year dispersion of r sampled on a matched δ/σ grid is <0.75× the dispersion on the fixed-tick grid. If true, the profitable operating point is a δ/σ constant and the registered H-006 adaptive-threshold variant has a theory-backed mechanism; if false, threshold optimality is cost-determined (δ* ≈ 2c/(r−1)) and H-006 should be demoted before any NT8 configs are spent on it.

**Mechanism.** N(δ) ∝ (σ/δ)² (scaling law / DC vol estimator, Petrov-Golub-Olsen 2019) makes fixed-tick event frequency ∝ σ², explaining H-006's −41% selectivity drift. Combining with fixed per-flip cost c gives Π(δ) ∝ σ²[(r(δ)−1)/δ − c/δ²]: if r is scale-invariant in ticks, δ* = 2c/(r−1) independent of σ (fixed ticks fine, just possibly mis-sized); if r is invariant in δ/σ, then δ* ∝ σ and only an adaptive threshold holds the operating point. The two regimes are empirically distinguishable from the same segment statistics [derivation is inference from cited laws].

**Experiment.** Python, same run family as DC01 (shared segment extraction, one spec). Compute r(δ) per calendar year on (a) the fixed tick grid {45…358} and (b) a δ/σ_y grid with σ_y = that year's median per-bar EWMA vol, choosing tick values per year so grid points match in δ/σ (quantized to ticks); second σ estimator (median daily range) as preregistered robustness twin. Dispersion metric D = mean over grid points of inter-year IQR of r. Also: from segment statistics alone, PREDICT the S1 spatial sweep's net-vs-δ ordering (Π(δ) formula with measured r and actual cost model) and compare to the realized S1 sweep results (already run — this is a genuine out-of-model validation of the theory).

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS (vol-invariance): D_vol < 0.75 × D_tick under BOTH σ estimators → H-006 NT8 variant is green-lit with k anchored so median δ_t = 179 ticks (anchor preregistered, lookback ∈ {10, 20} days only, per H-006's existing text). FAIL toward fixed-tick: D_vol ≥ D_tick under both estimators → H-006 demoted to low priority; the cost-determined δ* = 2c/(r−1) estimate is reported with CI and compared to 179. Ambiguous (estimators disagree): no promotion either way; record and stop. S1 cross-check (secondary, preregistered): Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6 between predicted Π(δ) ordering and realized S1 sweep ordering validates the framework; ρ < 0.3 flags the framework itself as inadequate for NQ closes.

**Priority:** high · **Degrees of freedom:** ~2: σ estimator family (2 preregistered variants, both must agree) and grid quantization rule. No trading-rule parameters; no NT8 runs consumed. · **Overfitting risk:** Low — this is model selection between two preregistered normalizations, decided by a single preregistered dispersion statistic; the danger of quietly tuning k for H-006 is explicitly deferred to the later NT8 experiment which already has its own registered gates.

### DR01-H3 (DC03_MULTISCALE_SCALEBREAK_TAG) — A multi-threshold DC scale-break statistic B — the count of 45-tick DC flips inside the trailing 120 bars, normalized by its σ²-implied expectation — stratifies next-Type-1-trade expectancy monotonically, and its top-vs-bottom quartile expectancy gap exceeds $30/trade AND survives controlling for the existing SW01 vol-tercile tag (i.e., it is incremental information, not a vol proxy).

**Mechanism.** Under BM, event rates at two thresholds are locked at (δ1/δ2)²; deviations measure local roughness/trendiness independent of overall vol level (intrinsic-network idea of Golub et al 2017; Tsang's regime profiling). SW01 found high-vol and high-flip regimes carry the P&L (chop-veto inverted); B is the DC-native, theoretically normalized version of that finding — if it subsumes/extends the vol tercile, it becomes the correct conditioning variable for the redesigned SW05 veto and for SW08 sizing.

**Experiment.** Tag-only Python pass over the SW01 bar+trade ledger (zero strategy changes, zero NT8 runs): compute B at each entry bar (window 120 bars and 179-segment-aligned variant — both preregistered), quartile-sort entries, report per-quartile net/trade, PF, and two-way sort against vol tercile; segment-block bootstrap for the gap. Only if PASS does a veto/sizing variant get DESIGNED (as a separate, later frontier item with its own gates, consuming NT8 configs).

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** PASS: monotone per-quartile expectancy (no inversions beyond bootstrap noise), top-vs-bottom gap > $30/trade with p<0.05 (segment-block bootstrap), AND the gap within at least 2 of 3 vol terciles retains the same sign with ≥50% of the unconditional magnitude. FAIL: non-monotone, gap ≤ $30, or gap vanishes inside vol terciles (then B is just volatility and adds nothing over SW01 tags — closed, and SW05/SW08 proceed on the existing tags).

**Priority:** medium · **Degrees of freedom:** 2 (small-δ = 45 ticks fixed by the δ/4 convention; two window definitions preregistered and both reported — no post-hoc selection permitted). · **Overfitting risk:** Moderate: this is a conditioning-variable search, the classic overfitting doorway. Contained by tag-only design (no rule built unless the preregistered gap gates pass), by requiring incrementality over already-known tags, and by the campaign's CPCV/PSR gates on any downstream veto variant.

### DR01-H4 (DC04_CLOSE_BASIS_DISCRETIZATION) — The close-basis trigger creates a material, state-dependent effective-threshold wedge: mean crossing excess E[ε] at flips exceeds 5 ticks ($25, comparable to the $25.4 full-history avg trade) or varies by >2× between the low- and high-vol terciles — meaning nominal δ=179 is not the threshold the system actually trades, and high-vol years run a systematically larger effective threshold with worse effective entry prices.

**Mechanism.** Sampling a continuous path at 1-min closes means the first close beyond the trigger overshoots it by ε ≥ 0, with E[ε] of order one per-bar sigma [inference; no DC-on-sampled-bars literature found — this is a gap our measurement fills]. ε acts as (a) extra execution drag per flip (enters the Π(δ) cost term c of DC02), (b) a vol-dependent widening of δ_eff that partially COUNTERACTS the H-006 selectivity drift and must be included before judging tick-vs-vol invariance, and (c) the quantitative form of RE01's 'single-point path noise'.

**Experiment.** Python on the SW01 bar ledger, same run family as DC01: at every flip record ε = |close − (anchor ∓ 179 ticks)|; distribution by year and vol tercile; additionally, using bar High/Low, count flips where the intrabar extreme crossed the trigger ≥1 bar before the close-cross bar and measure the foregone entry improvement in ticks (upper bound on what an intrabar-trigger variant could recover, before wick-noise costs).

**Pass/fail (preregistered).** Decision rule preregistered: if mean ε > 5 ticks OR tercile ratio E[ε|high]/E[ε|low] > 2 → (i) all DC01/DC02 analyses must be re-read using δ_eff = δ + E[ε|state] (re-reads preregistered in the same spec), and (ii) an intrabar-trigger NinjaScript variant (new class name per hot-reload rule) is added to the frontier BACKLOG as a candidate — not run in Wave 1. If neither condition holds → wedge is immaterial; closed permanently, one sentence in the registry. Intrabar upper-bound read: if median foregone improvement < 1 tick, the intrabar variant is rejected without ever being built.

**Priority:** medium · **Degrees of freedom:** 0 tuned (thresholds 5 ticks / 2× / 1 tick fixed here, before any data is seen). · **Overfitting risk:** Very low — bounded measurement with a mechanical decision rule; its main value is preventing a false verdict in DC02 and pre-empting a speculative NT8 variant with a free upper-bound calculation.

## Decision

ADMIT TO ACTIVE FRONTIER NOW: DC01 (overshoot decomposition + hazard) and DC02 (threshold-invariance test), run as one Python measurement family on already-archived ledgers — zero NT8 configs, zero fresh-data consumption, preregistered reads. Rationale: the literature's single deepest result for our system is that the celebrated overshoot scaling law (E[overshoot] ≈ threshold) is precisely the martingale no-alpha null — our stop-and-reverse P&L per flip is identically (overshoot − threshold − costs), so the entire campaign edge is a measurable ~3% deviation of NQ's overshoot ratio from 1.0. DC01 converts that from framing into attribution (which years/regimes/directions carry r>1), doubles as an independent integrity reconciliation of the NT8 ledger, and its hazard read arbitrates the thesis §7 exit debate and the registered H-007 with zero new degrees of freedom. DC02 answers the packet's central theory question — optimal threshold vs volatility — by testing which normalization (ticks vs vol units) makes the overshoot-ratio curve regime-invariant; it directly gates the already-registered H-006 adaptive-threshold NT8 experiment before any configs are spent, and its Π(δ) prediction is validated out-of-model against the already-run S1 spatial sweep. ADMIT CONDITIONALLY: DC03 (multi-scale scale-break tag) as a tag-only pass — it formalizes SW01's inverted chop-veto finding in DC-native form but is a conditioning-variable search, so it stays tag-only behind hard incrementality gates. RUN ALONGSIDE DC01 AT NEAR-ZERO COST: DC04 (close-basis discretization wedge), which protects DC02's verdict and cheaply kills or justifies any future intrabar-trigger variant. DO NOT IMPORT: Alpha-Engine-style counter-trend coastline trading (wrong family, unreviewed, cost-infeasible on 1-lot NQ) and all DC+ML profitability claims (FX, selection-biased); the filter-rule/PnF record (Fama-Blume 1966, Anderson-Faff 2008) should be logged in the registry as the historical base rate against fixed-reversal SAR alpha — it justifies the campaign's existing posture that survival of the cost gates, not gross profitability, is the binding test. One structural adoption for the protocol: DC segments are the theoretically correct blocking unit — the trend-episode bootstrap and CPCV blocking should be defined on DC segments/episodes rather than calendar days where feasible (Guillaume 1997 / Volume Clock lineage), which the existing SW01 episode tags already support.
