Solar Wave RK Research Thesis for a Robust NQ System
Evidence standard used here
I separate four kinds of statements throughout:
•	Documented mechanics: what the Solar Wave manual says the indicator does.
•	User-supplied evidence: your current NinjaTrader backtests.
•	External evidence: academic or official sources about trend persistence, market microstructure, volatility management, validation, and platform behavior.
•	Research hypotheses: plausible improvements that still require controlled testing.
The central conclusion is:
Solar Wave RK should be treated as the prospective directional-expansion core of the NQ system—not as the complete system.
It is a coherent, low-dimensional, positively skewed trend engine. Its main weakness is not that it lacks historical profitability; it is that its historical edge is thin, its losses cluster during non-trending periods, its fixed-tick scale may not remain appropriate across volatility regimes, and multiple Solar Wave variants do not constitute genuine diversification.
The path to better future Sharpe and lower drawdown is therefore:
Preserve Solar Wave’s large-trend payoff
        +
remove clearly unproductive repeated exposure
        +
scale its risk more consistently
        +
add a genuinely different failed-persistence/value-reversion engine
        +
control both families through one portfolio risk layer
That is a better objective than trying to force Solar Wave itself into an unnaturally smooth, always-profitable shape.

What Solar Wave fundamentally contributes
Solar Wave is best interpreted as a dual-speed, path-dependent trend state machine with hysteresis.
Its slower TrailingStop determines whether the directional regime remains valid. Its faster TrendVector distinguishes a progressing trend from a correction or mature trend. The manual recommends that the trailing-stop offset be approximately twice the Trend Vector offset, which closely matches your 179 / 90 ≈ 1.99 configuration.
That gives the system two distinct questions:a
90-tick inner threshold:
    Is the current trend still healthy?

179-tick outer threshold:
    Is the trend itself still valid?
Combined with five-bar slowdown confirmation and ten-bar separation between corrections and pullbacks, Solar Wave filters both price noise and time noise.
Your backtests suggest that this state representation contains information. All three signal families were profitable after commissions in the current historical assumptions, and Type 1 produced the best initial balance of PF, average trade, turnover, and drawdown.

2. Economic model of the Solar Wave edge
2.1 What behavior is Solar Wave monetizing?
Solar Wave monetizes:
Directional price persistence after movement has exceeded a meaningful spatial threshold and survived a temporal persistence test.
It is not forecasting the next minute. It accepts that it will enter late and lose on false starts. Its expected payoff is:
Many failed or incomplete trends
        +
A smaller number of sustained directional moves
        =
Positive expectancy if the large moves are large enough
Trend-following evidence exists across equity-index, bond, commodity, and currency futures, although the classic evidence concerns longer horizons than your one-minute implementation. Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen documented time-series return persistence across 58 futures and forward markets; Hurst, Ooi, and Pedersen extended trend-following evidence across more than a century. These papers support the broad economic premise, not Solar Wave’s specific parameters or intraday NQ profitability. (SSRN)
Intraday persistence has also been documented. Research on market intraday momentum found that earlier market returns can predict later-session returns, with stronger effects on high-volatility, high-volume, recession, and major-news days. Separate futures research links late-day momentum partly to hedging demand, including short-gamma hedging that requires trading in the direction of price movement. Again, these findings do not validate Solar Wave directly, but they make intraday trend persistence economically plausible. (SSRN)
2.2 Plausible mechanisms behind intraday NQ persistence
These mechanisms are not mutually exclusive, and none has been proven to explain your exact backtest.
Mechanism	How it may create persistence	Confidence
Institutional order splitting	Large orders are executed gradually to limit market impact, creating persistent same-direction order flow	Strong general microstructure evidence
Gradual information diffusion	Participants absorb and act on information at different speeds, producing initial underreaction	Strong theoretical and broader empirical foundation
Hedging and rebalancing	Short-gamma dealers, leveraged products, volatility-control strategies, and portfolio rebalancers may trade with the move	Credible; varies by regime
Liquidity withdrawal	During shocks, liquidity providers widen or withdraw, allowing directional orders to move price farther	Credible microstructure mechanism
Stop cascades	Threshold breaks activate stop orders and breakout flows	Plausible; difficult to measure without order data
Behavioral herding	Traders react to observed movement and reinforce it	Plausible; not independently identified in your data
Global price discovery	NQ reacts during Asia, Europe, U.S. premarket, macro releases, and the cash session	Structurally relevant because NQ is nearly 24-hour
Delayed positioning	Investors cannot or do not rebalance instantaneously	Supported by intraday-momentum models
Order-sign persistence and long memory have been linked to order splitting, while market-impact research shows that sequences of large same-direction orders can generate persistent directional pressure. (APS Journals)
Hong and Stein’s underreaction model shows how gradually diffusing information can initially produce trend continuation before later overreaction or reversal. It is a broader asset-pricing model, not an NQ-specific explanation. (Columbia University)
Options-related hedging can either amplify or damp market moves depending on aggregate gamma positioning. Research on intraday futures momentum and Cboe analysis of index-option hedging both describe direction-following hedging when dealers are short gamma. (SSRN)
2.3 Why hysteresis may outperform one simple crossover
A single fast crossover tends to alternate between:
trend
not trend
trend
not trend
whenever price oscillates around one threshold.
Solar Wave instead has:
Inner boundary:
    strong versus weak

Outer boundary:
    current trend versus reversed trend
This reduces “chattering.” A correction may change the trend from strong to weak without requiring a full directional reversal.
The structure is analogous to a control system that requires more evidence to reverse state than to admit that current momentum has weakened.
That is particularly appropriate for trend following because normal trends contain retracements. A system that declares every retracement to be a new trend will repeatedly buy highs and sell lows.
2.4 Why the roughly 2:1 ratio is structurally sensible
The ratio is vendor guidance, not a mathematical law. The manual says the trailing-stop distance should roughly double the Trend Vector distance.
The practical logic is:
Small correction:
    touches or flattens Trend Vector
    trend becomes weak
    direction remains intact

Larger structural reversal:
    reaches TrailingStop
    trend direction flips
If both offsets were almost equal, strength and direction would change together, eliminating the useful weak-trend state.
If the trailing offset were many times larger, the strategy might survive every correction but return excessive profit and accept very large losses when the trend finally failed.
A roughly 2:1 hierarchy is therefore a plausible compromise between:
•	preserving normal corrections;
•	reacting to genuine reversals;
•	allowing a meaningful pullback zone;
•	retaining positive skew.
2.5 Why the recent historical period may have supported Solar Wave
The following are inferences, not yet established through your trade-level data:
1.	NQ regularly experiences large repricing episodes around macro information, technology-sector news, earnings, rates, and risk sentiment.
2.	Its nearly 24-hour trading creates multiple distinct windows of price discovery.
3.	The one-minute 90/179 configuration may have been well scaled to recent NQ intraday volatility.
4.	Large index moves can produce a few highly profitable trades that pay for many false starts.
5.	One NQ contract carries large dollar exposure, so high nominal profits do not necessarily imply a high statistical edge.
Your aggregate PF illustrates this last point. With rounded PF of 1.13 and net profit of $146,441, Type 1’s implied gross loss is approximately:
Gross loss ≈ $146,441 / (1.13 − 1)
           ≈ $1.13 million

Gross profit ≈ $1.27 million
That is only an approximation because PF was rounded. It nevertheless shows that the strategy’s margin between gross profit and gross loss is small relative to total turnover.
2.6 Why it may decay
Solar Wave could weaken if:
•	NQ becomes more mean-reverting intraday;
•	market-making capacity increases relative to directional flow;
•	trends occur primarily as instantaneous gaps rather than persistent movement;
•	execution costs increase;
•	more market participants trade similar fast trend rules;
•	the fixed offsets become inappropriate for the volatility environment;
•	price moves remain large but reverse too quickly;
•	session structure changes;
•	the few very large trends become less frequent.
The external literature documents trend persistence broadly but does not imply that every horizon, market, parameterization, or period remains profitable forever. (ScienceDirect)

The manual’s page-3 ladder-rung diagrams explain that Trend Step measures how well a trend recovers after a correction; smaller and consecutively decreasing steps are presented as evidence of trend maturation. The manual also warns that a mature trend may produce one final powerful move before reversing.
That warning is crucial:
Trend maturity should first be used to block late new entries—not automatically to exit an existing profitable runner.

5.1 Episode-controlled core with one Type 3 re-entry
1.	Name: Type 1 Core + Single Same-Episode Type 3 Re-entry.
2.	Falsifiable hypothesis: The first same-direction Type 3 after a premature Type 1 exit has positive incremental expectancy when the Solar trend direction and episode have not changed.

Enter Type 1 once per trend episode.

If:
    position exits at a loss or small gain
    AND Signal_Trend direction does not flip
    AND trend episode is unchanged
    AND no re-entry has occurred
    AND same-direction Type 3 appears
Then:
    permit one re-entry
6.	Expected return: Moderately higher if genuine trends frequently resume after washouts.
7.	Expected Sharpe: Potentially higher, provided the re-entry sleeve itself is profitable after costs.
8.	Expected maximum drawdown: Could fall if re-entry recovers premature losses; could rise if it compounds failing-trend exposure.
9.	Expected drawdown duration: Potentially shorter during genuine trends.
10.	Skew and large-winner retention: Should preserve or improve positive skew because it restores exposure to trends.
11.	Average trade and costs: Lower than Type 1 alone but should be materially better than unconditional Type 3.
12.	Turnover and exposure: Moderate increase; strictly capped.
13.	Correlation with baseline: Very high. This is a refinement, not diversification.
14.	Works in: Deep correction followed by continued trend.
15.	Fails in: Mature trends, delayed reversals, repeated fake resumptions.
16.	Degrees of freedom: Maximum re-entries, permitted exit reasons, minimum bars after exit, maximum distance from stop.

6. Multi-timeframe and MA-context assessment
6.1 Timeframe changes are not simple noise reduction
With identical parameters:
Parameter	1-minute	2-minute	3-minute	5-minute
Slowdown Scan = 5	5 min	10 min	15 min	25 min
Weak-Weak Split = 10	10 min	20 min	30 min	50 min
Pullback Split = 10	10 min	20 min	30 min	50 min
Trend offset = 90 ticks	22.5 pts	22.5 pts	22.5 pts	22.5 pts
Stop offset = 179 ticks	44.75 pts	44.75 pts	44.75 pts	44.75 pts
The spatial parameters remain fixed, but the temporal parameters become much slower.
A five-minute version with the same settings is therefore not merely a smoother one-minute strategy. It is a different state machine:
•	signals are evaluated less frequently;
•	slowdown confirmation takes five times longer;
•	pullback separation takes five times longer;
•	bar highs and lows aggregate more movement;
•	entry and exit timing changes;
•	the count and identity of trends change.
6.2 Correct staged timeframe research
Stage A: raw portability
Run:
1m, 2m, 3m, 5m
with identical 90/179/5/10/10 settings
Question:
Does the original configuration naturally generalize?
Stage B: elapsed-time normalization
Approximate comparable timing:
Bar period	Slowdown	Weak/Pullback split
1m	5	10
2m	3	5
3m	2	3
5m	1	2
This is only approximate because integer bars cannot perfectly preserve elapsed time.
Question:
Does a different price-sampling interval improve the signal when temporal meaning is held roughly constant?
Stage C: multi-horizon context
Use a slower timeframe as a veto or context, not necessarily a mandatory entry condition.
For example:
Type 1:
    allowed unless the slow trend is strongly opposed

Type 2 and Type 3:
    require slow trend alignment
The manual recommends a major/minor approach in which minor-trend entries are taken only when aligned with a slower major trend.
That is a coherent hypothesis, but the manual’s example is Renko-based and does not prove that the same structure improves NQ one-minute results.
6.3 MA context: when it helps
A 20/50/100 versus 200 EMA stack can help identify:
•	broad primary direction;
•	whether local Solar signals oppose established drift;
•	whether a trend is early, established, or mature;
•	whether averages are compressed and unstable.
The useful information is not just alignment:
Direction:
    fast averages above/below EMA 200

Separation:
    how far apart they are relative to volatility

Slope:
    whether EMA 200 and faster averages are actually moving

Stability:
    how often the alignment state recently changed
6.4 When MA context enters too late
Strictly requiring:
EMA20 > EMA200
EMA50 > EMA200
EMA100 > EMA200
before every long Type 1 can reject the best early trend transitions.
The likely sequence in a fast reversal is:
Solar Type 1
    first

EMA20 confirmation
    later

EMA50 confirmation
    still later

EMA100 confirmation
    latest
A strict rule may improve PF and win rate simply because it takes fewer, later trades. It may also remove the largest portion of the right tail.
6.5 Recommended MA role
The initial test should be:
Strongly opposed MA state:
    veto Type 2 and Type 3
    possibly veto Type 1

Mixed or transitional MA state:
    permit Type 1 only

Stable full alignment:
    permit Type 1
    permit selective Type 2
    permit controlled Type 3
MA context should first be recorded as a diagnostic tag. It should not immediately become four additional optimized thresholds.
6.6 One robust timeframe versus an ensemble
A 1m, 2m, 3m, and 5m Solar ensemble may appear diversified but will likely share:
•	the same major NQ trends;
•	the same directional positions;
•	the same large winning days;
•	the same range losses.
Treat all versions as one family unless empirical position overlap and drawdown overlap show otherwise.
Choosing one robust timeframe may be safer than averaging several correlated variants. A consensus approach becomes justified only if the versions show materially different trade timing and improved family-level risk after an aggregate exposure cap.

7. Exit and right-tail preservation
Core principle
A Solar refinement is not successful if it raises PF mainly by deleting the few trades that pay for the strategy’s false starts.
Trend-following research generally finds positive skew and long-run persistence, while practitioner research also highlights a possible trade-off between maximizing Sharpe and retaining the positive-skew characteristics of trend following. (Research Affiliates)

Catastrophe-stop role
A catastrophe stop should be outside normal Solar trade management.
Conceptually:
Normal exit:
    Solar trailing invalidation

Emergency exit:
    extreme adverse movement,
    data failure,
    gap,
    or abnormal volatility
The hard stop should be evaluated using MAE percentiles, not optimized solely for net profit.
A sensible experiment is:
No emergency stop
200 ticks
260 ticks
320 ticks

Why fast breakeven is dangerous

2.	retrace;
3.	test entry;
4.	resume.
An immediate breakeven rule can create a pattern of:

stop moved to entry
normal correction
flat exit
large trend without position
This may make the loss distribution look cleaner while materially weakening geometric growth.
Session-close decision
You need a matched-trade counterfactual:
For every session-close exit:
    What would have happened under continued Solar management?
Measure:
•	overnight MAE;
•	next-session MFE;
•	gap distribution;
•	profit retained;
•	added expected shortfall;
•	effect on average holding;
•	effect on total portfolio overnight exposure.
Do not decide from aggregate net profit alone.

8. Volatility normalization and position sizing
8.1 Least invasive order of operations
The safest progression is:
1. Fixed Solar signals + fixed one-contract baseline
2. Fixed Solar signals + volatility-adjusted sizing
3. Fixed signals + broad volatility eligibility
4. Two discrete signal regimes
5. Continuous ATR-adaptive Solar parameters, only much later
Changing position size preserves the original alpha hypothesis. Changing 90/179 dynamically changes which events count as trends.
8.2 Useful diagnostics
Record for every entry:
TrendOffset / ATR60
StopOffset / ATR60
TrendOffset / trailing 60-minute realized range
StopOffset / trailing 60-minute realized range
Current volatility percentile
Same-time-of-day volatility percentile
Interpretation:
Very low ratio:
    offsets are tight relative to market movement
    more flips and noise expected

Very high ratio:
    offsets are wide relative to available movement
    late signals and poor reward-to-cost expected
8.3 Risk sizing
For one contract:
Dollar risk ≈ stop distance in points × $20
Using MNQ permits roughly 0.1-NQ increments.
A conservative sizing framework would be:
Desired dollar risk per trade
    /
Estimated catastrophe-stop dollar risk per MNQ
    =
MNQ quantity
Then apply:
•	family-level maximum;
•	portfolio-level maximum;
•	volatility-spike cap;
•	minimum and maximum number of MNQ contracts;
•	no leverage increase merely because recent volatility is unusually low.
8.4 Volatility target
A portfolio volatility target should be selected only after:
•	reliable daily mark-to-market equity exists;
•	strategy-family correlations are measured;
•	stress drawdowns are estimated;
•	forward behavior is observed.
Potential research targets such as 6%, 8%, 10%, and 12% annualized volatility may be tested, but they are not recommendations.
8.5 Kelly criterion
Kelly sizing maximizes expected logarithmic growth under accurately known probabilities and distributions, but full Kelly can generate severe short-term drawdowns and is highly sensitive to estimation error. Fractional or risk-constrained Kelly methods trade some growth for smaller drawdown risk. (Department of Statistics)
For this system:
•	full Kelly is inappropriate;
•	estimated Kelly should be treated as an upper-bound diagnostic;
•	any future allocation should be constrained by volatility, expected shortfall, and stress drawdown;
•	leverage should be applied to the diversified portfolio, not the best Solar variant.

9.1 Best first diversifier: failed breakout plus value reacceptance
The first complementary strategy should not be generic:
Price is far from VWAP
→ fade it
It should require objective evidence that directional movement failed.
A first rule concept:
Define a fixed opening range.

Price closes outside the range.
The breakout fails to extend.
Price closes back inside the range within a limited time.
Price is reaccepted toward session value/VWAP.
Solar Wave is not in a strong same-direction trend.

Enter toward:
    VWAP
    or opening-range midpoint

Stop:
    beyond the failed extreme

Maximum:
    one trade per side per session
This monetizes a different behavior from Solar:
Solar:
    movement persists

Failed breakout:
    movement is rejected
Research has documented intraday reversals after large opening moves in U.S. stock-index futures, and separate work documents overnight-to-intraday reversal effects across futures. These findings support testing the family but do not validate this exact NQ rule. (ScienceDirect)
9.2 Why it can improve portfolio Sharpe
During Solar’s worst environment:
Type 1 breakout
→ no continuation
→ price returns into value
→ opposite Type 1
→ another failure
A failed-breakout strategy may profit from the return toward value.
That could reduce:
•	losing-day overlap;
•	drawdown overlap;
•	time under water;
•	dependence on trend years.
9.3 Why it can be dangerous
Mean reversion is often negatively skewed:
many modest winners
+
occasional large loss during a true trend
A mean-reversion strategy must not repeatedly short a strong Solar uptrend or buy a strong Solar downtrend.
Suggested router logic:
Solar strong + high efficiency:
    trend family has priority
    opposing mean reversion blocked

Solar weak + failed extension + value reacceptance:
    mean reversion may activate

Conflicting ambiguous state:
    no trade
9.4 Overnight inventory correction
Research has found overnight-intraday reversal patterns across asset classes and futures. More recent work on U.S. market indices reports that overnight-to-opening reversal has varied over time and weakened in some post-2010 periods. That makes it an interesting but lower-priority candidate. (SSRN)
Its advantage is clear timing and low overlap:
Signal:
    overnight imbalance

Trading window:
    first part of U.S. session

Exit:
    return toward prior close, VWAP, or defined time
Its weakness is that it can oppose a genuine opening trend.
9.5 ORB continuation is not the best first diversifier
An ORB continuation strategy may be valuable, but economically it resembles Solar:
range break
→ continuation
→ trailing exit
It should be considered another directional-expansion subfamily unless its drawdowns and positions prove materially distinct.

Use at least two geometries:

B. 18 months train / 6 months test / 3-month step
The candidate-selection rule must be fixed before the test folds.
Purge trades that cross fold boundaries and add an embargo at least as long as the maximum intended holding horizon or one full session, whichever is longer.
12.7 Parameter plateau
A candidate is preferable when:
central parameter works
AND
most immediate neighbors work
AND
neighbor median is close to central result
Suggested gate:
At least 60% of immediate neighbors pass basic economics
Neighbor median Calmar >= 80% of center
No cliff-like collapse under one small parameter change
12.8 Multiple testing
Record every:
•	hypothesis;
•	strategy variation;
•	parameter set;
•	timeframe;
•	session rule;
•	exit;
•	metric used for selection.
The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for selection bias, non-normality, and multiple testing. The Probability of Backtest Overfitting framework estimates the chance that an in-sample winner underperforms out of sample. (SSRN)
For a large research program, also consider:
•	White’s Reality Check;
•	Hansen’s Superior Predictive Ability test;
•	comparison against the frozen Type 1 benchmark.
These procedures were developed specifically to account for data snooping across many competing models. (Users SSC Wisconsin)
12.9 Bootstrap and Monte Carlo
Do not simply reshuffle individual Solar trades. Solar losses and winners cluster by trend episode and market regime.
Use:
•	day-block bootstrap;
•	trend-episode bootstrap;
•	stationary bootstrap of daily returns;
•	scenario injection for gaps and slippage.
The stationary bootstrap was designed to preserve dependence in weakly dependent time-series observations. (JSTOR)
12.10 Large-winner concentration
Run:
Original
Remove largest 1 trade
Remove largest 3
Remove largest 5
Remove largest 10
Cap each trade at 95th percentile
Do not require Solar to remain highly profitable after removing all large winners—that would reject the fundamental nature of trend following.
Instead ask:
•	Is the refined strategy more concentrated than baseline?
•	Does one accidental trade explain the claimed improvement?
•	Are large winners distributed across years and directions?
•	Does the strategy retain positive expectancy after removing only the single largest trade?

15. Red-team critique
1. Are proposed filters merely reducing exposure?
Possibly. Every filter must be compared at:
•	equal realized volatility;
•	equal market exposure;
•	equal trade count where meaningful;
•	net profit per exposed hour.
A filter is not automatically valuable because absolute drawdown falls.
2. Is recent NQ unusually favorable?
Possibly. The sample is short relative to structural regime change and has already been repeatedly inspected. That is why the research must emphasize rolling tests and locked forward rather than full-sample PF.
3. Could improvements destroy the right tail?
Yes. The most dangerous ideas are:
•	strict slow-trend confirmation;
•	breakeven;
•	tight hard stop;
•	maximum holding;
•	partial profit;
•	aggressive giveback lock.
The revised plan uses maturity measures mainly to block new entries, not liquidate the core.
4. Are multiple Solar versions false diversification?
Almost certainly until proven otherwise.
They should share one family risk cap.
5. Is the proposed Solar architecture becoming too complex?
It could. The maximum acceptable core should remain approximately:
Type 1
One Type 3 re-entry
One selective Type 2
One chop state
One volatility sizing rule
One catastrophe stop
Any additional module must displace or simplify an existing one.
6. Are the context filters causal?
Not necessarily. MA alignment, ADX, efficiency, and volatility may merely correlate with historical winners.
Preference should be given to:
•	recent Solar flip frequency;
•	wave/episode state;
•	path efficiency;
•	normalized volatility.
These connect directly to identified failure modes.
7. Could historical bar information leak into exits?
Yes. In particular:
•	using a current bar’s completed high/low to update a stop applied within the same bar;
•	historical early pullback signals;
•	ambiguous stop versus exit ordering.
Playback and higher-fill-resolution checks are mandatory.
8. Could mean reversion erase Solar gains?
Yes. The failed-breakout strategy must require confirmed failure and value reacceptance, not merely “Solar is weak.”
9. Could volatility targeting hide poor alpha?
Yes. Report unscaled strategy expectancy separately from scaled performance.
10. Could Sharpe improve because fewer trades make the estimate unstable?
Yes. Minimum trade counts and temporal coverage are hard gates.
11. Are 90/179 implicitly fitted to recent volatility?
Possibly. Diagnose offset-to-volatility ratios before changing them.
12. Could one gap exceed historical drawdown?
Yes. Bar-based historical maximum drawdown is not a worst-case bound.
Use:
•	gap scenarios;
•	stop-through assumptions;
•	exchange-halt scenarios;
•	data-disconnect scenarios;
•	margin stress.
13. Is the final portfolio still one NQ bet?
It will be unless the complementary family earns from genuinely different behavior and has low losing-day/drawdown overlap.
14. Which recommendation should be removed first?
The first to remove would be a strict multi-MA or full higher-timeframe alignment gate.
It is likely redundant, lagging, and capable of deleting the early part of the right tail. It remains worth diagnostic tagging and a soft-veto test, but it should not be part of the first core architecture.

1.	The objective is robust portfolio-level geometric growth, not maximum backtest profit.
2.	Solar Wave’s positive right tail must be explicitly measured and protected.
3.	One experiment tests one falsifiable hypothesis.
