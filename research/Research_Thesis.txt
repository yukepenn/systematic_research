3. Executive verdict
What the campaign accomplished well
The campaign appears to have produced three genuinely valuable outputs:
1.	A transparent open directional-change model.
The original commercial indicator was reduced to an interpretable state machine rather than left as a black-box optimization object.
2.	A strong negative result about parameter selection.
The campaign found that selecting one “best” threshold is unreliable and that broad ensembles are more defensible than historical argmax selection.
3.	An unusually candid record of failure.
Type 2 additions, general Type 3 re-entry, split exit/reversal, raw High/Low anchors, price scaling, timed exits, wave filters, and ES portability were rejected rather than quietly removed from the narrative. (GitHub)
What the campaign did not accomplish
It did not produce:
•	a clean out-of-sample result;
•	a statistically certified winner;
•	evidence that R5 is better than R4;
•	a directly executable 13-member futures implementation;
•	a second independent alpha family;
•	a portfolio;
•	a router;
•	locked-forward evidence;
•	anything that is ready for leverage or deployment.
The repository itself states plainly that no portfolio was built and that failed-persistence, value-reacceptance, session/inventory, and other families were never implemented. (GitHub)
Bottom-line classification
The project is at Level 2: a robust historical research candidate with a mechanically interpretable core.
It is not yet at Level 3 because there is no demonstrated independent second family. It is not at Level 4 because there is no real multi-family portfolio. It is not at Level 5 because no frozen forward period exists.
Strongest current conclusion
R5 is the strongest reported historical risk-adjusted research ensemble, but:
•	it has not been independently reexecuted here;
•	it is not statistically distinguishable from R4;
•	it is not yet represented as an executable discrete futures position process;
•	it remains heavily dependent on a handful of large days;
•	it failed the only completed external market portability test.
The more defensible conclusion is:
R5 and the broad fixed R4 ensemble should remain a two-candidate Pareto set until executable implementation, exact reconstruction, and new-market evidence distinguish them.

What the repository discloses
The README states:

•	approximately 316 configurations were ultimately run;
•	experiments.yaml contains only 2 of roughly 12 experiment entries;

•	383 ledgers when cost-stress and related reruns are included. (GitHub)
These counts may represent different denominators rather than a direct contradiction, but the exact trial universe is not independently reconstructable from the reports alone.
Consequences by statistic
PBO
PBO can still describe a parameter grid if the complete grid and all performance series are present. But it does not fully account for:
•	hypotheses that were tried but not recorded;
•	changes in candidate families;
•	changed metrics;
•	changed ensemble bounds;
•	post-result specification corrections.
Therefore, the reported PBO range is useful evidence that single-cell selection is dangerous, but it is not a complete campaign-level probability of overfitting.
DSR
DSR explicitly depends on the number and distribution of trials. Missing trial lineage makes the result assumption-sensitive. The campaign itself reports DSR around 0.45–0.55 under one trial convention and approximately 0.96 under another variance-pool interpretation, meaning the result is dominated by accounting assumptions rather than data. The correct conclusion is inconclusive, not passed or failed. (GitHub)
DSR was developed specifically to adjust Sharpe for multiple testing, selection bias, and non-normality. If the trial universe is incomplete, the correction cannot be considered definitive. (DOI)
The p = 0.009 volatility-control claim
The finalist report calls this the campaign’s only clean preregistered significance result. Yet the README says Waves 1c–3 do not have verifiable preregistration. Unless Git history proves that the volatility-versus-price hypothesis and pass criteria predated the output, the phrase “preregistered control” is too strong. (GitHub)
The statistical result may still be real as an exploratory paired test, but its evidentiary grade should be reduced.

Important weighting correction
The report calls R5 “equal risk,” but ensembles.py defines the ensemble as a strict fixed 1/N arithmetic mean, includes flat days as zero, and explicitly prohibits reweighting. That is equal member weight, not necessarily equal ex-ante or realized risk. (GitHub)
Those descriptions are equivalent only if all members are deliberately normalized to the same risk before averaging. The visible source does not establish that.
The language should be changed to:
strict equal-weight ensemble of one-contract member return streams
unless a separate member-risk normalization is demonstrated.
The deeper executability issue
A 1/13 mean of 13 one-contract NQ PnL series is a valid research return stream, but it is not directly executable with NQ contracts. A real implementation needs to convert member positions into one net NQ-equivalent target and then use integer NQ/MNQ contracts. MNQ has one-tenth the multiplier of NQ, providing finer—but still discrete—position granularity. (CME Group)
The executable process should be:
member positions ∈ {-1, 0, +1}
        ↓
mean member position
        ↓
target NQ-equivalent exposure
        ↓
round through NQ/MNQ contract constraints
        ↓
trade only changes in the net target
        ↓
apply actual commissions and slippage to those net executions
This may improve costs through internal signal netting, or worsen performance through rounding, additional MNQ costs, and inability to hold exact fractions. Until this is tested, the reported R5 stream is a theoretical ensemble return stream, not a finalized executable strategy.

10. Right-tail and geometric-growth assessment
The dominant fact is not R5’s 0.977 Sharpe. It is that:
•	the top 1% of trades contribute 160% of net;
•	the bottom 99% lose in aggregate;
•	removing the ten best days leaves only 36% of R5’s net;
•	one extra tick per execution materially reduces expectancy;
•	two-tick stress reportedly halves net;
•	three ticks may erase the edge. (GitHub)
This does not invalidate the system. It is mechanically consistent with positive-convexity trend following.
It does mean that:
1.	A profit target is probably destructive.
2.	A tight breakeven is probably destructive.
3.	A volatility veto can be destructive if it removes the largest expansion days.
4.	A mean-reversion strategy must not fight the top Solar days.
5.	Sizing must be able to survive long stretches where the ordinary trades lose.
6.	Geometric growth depends on retaining rare winners while controlling the cumulative cost of false starts.
Academic trend-following evidence supports persistence across many futures markets, but most classic evidence uses much longer horizons than this strategy. It establishes broad plausibility, not proof for three-minute NQ directional changes. (CBS Research Portal)
Intraday futures momentum has also been linked to hedging demand, including direction-following hedging under negative gamma, but those effects are regime-dependent and do not establish a permanent NQ edge. (RePub)

11. Scientific interpretation
Directional-change framework
Directional-change methods sample price by event thresholds rather than fixed clock time. Research in foreign exchange has found event-based scaling relationships and has developed multi-threshold intrinsic networks, supporting the general idea that event-time representations can reveal structure hidden by clock-time sampling. (IDEAS/RePEc)
That literature does not prove the campaign’s NQ edge. It supports treating directional changes, overshoots, reversals, and threshold hierarchies as legitimate scientific objects rather than arbitrary technical-analysis patterns.
Plausible mechanism
A coherent interpretation is:
Large participant acquires or unwinds risk
        ↓
Order is split through time
        ↓
Order-flow signs remain persistent
        ↓
Price moves directionally until liquidity or opposing flow absorbs it
        ↓
Directional-change filter enters after persistence is observable
        ↓
Rare long overshoots pay for false reversals
Order-sign long memory is widely documented, and order splitting is a leading microscopic explanation. (APS Journals)
The strategy does not need to identify which mechanism is active on every trade. It needs only that directional persistence occurs frequently and strongly enough to overcome its false-start and execution costs.
Why the edge may disappear
The edge should deteriorate when:
•	directional order flow becomes shorter-lived;
•	volatility remains too low relative to fixed execution costs;
•	moves occur as instantaneous gaps rather than tradable overshoots;
•	mean reversion becomes faster;
•	market makers absorb flow more efficiently;
•	the top-tail expansion events become less frequent;
•	live slippage exceeds the historical assumption.
The repository already identifies low volatility, right-tail drought, execution beyond two ticks, and overshoot ratio returning to one as economic invalidators. (GitHub)

12. Second independent red team
12.1 Strongest objections
1. The ensemble is not yet an executable futures strategy
Strict averaging of full-contract PnL streams is mathematically convenient but does not establish that an integer NQ/MNQ implementation produces the same:
•	returns;
•	costs;
•	turnover;
•	drawdown;
•	tail exposure.
This is the most important missing implementation test.
2. Published “average trade” is misleading
R5’s $5.80 per ensemble unit is not an ordinary executable NQ trade statistic. It is net PnL divided across fractional member trades. Comparing it directly with full-contract round-trip costs is not meaningful until member positions are netted and discretized.
3. “Equal risk” is not established
The visible source implements equal member weight. Different thresholds can have different volatility, turnover, and tail exposure. Equal weight is not automatically equal risk. (GitHub)
4. The absolute-edge bootstrap is conditional on a selected model
absolute_edge_bootstrap() resamples the selected series using a fixed 20-day circular block. It estimates uncertainty conditional on that series; it does not correct for the entire strategy-selection process. The block length is also a modeling choice. (GitHub)
5. Mark-to-market may be overstated as an achieved capability
The code visibly buckets PnL by exit calendar date. Without bar-level open-position equity, the published drawdown and ES may not represent true capital risk.
6. Five positive “years” is four full years plus part of 2026
That is encouraging but should not be described as five complete calendar years. (GitHub)
7. R5 superiority is not established
R5 versus R4 is a ranking preference based on point estimates and mechanism, not a statistical conclusion. The campaign admits this explicitly. (GitHub)
8. The short side may be insurance, not merely dead weight
Shorts have weak standalone average performance, but they may carry crisis convexity. Removing them should be judged by:
•	expected shortfall;
•	worst weeks;
•	2022 behavior;
•	portfolio marginal contribution;
not just average short PnL.
9. ES failure is load-bearing
A mechanism that does not transfer to a closely related equity-index future deserves a substantial overfitting penalty. One failed market is not conclusive, but it prevents claims of broadly portable structural alpha.
10. The campaign stopped before the most important portfolio work
The user’s true objective is a smoother multi-family compounding system. The campaign’s formal stop condition was sensible for the Solar refinement frontier, but it should not be treated as proof that the complementary-strategy frontier has low value. The portfolio report explicitly says that frontier was never started. (GitHub)
11. Registry failure weakens the cleanest significance claim
The asserted preregistered volatility-control result needs commit-level chronology before it can be called preregistered.
12. The public vendor-binary incident is unresolved governance risk
Research quality includes provenance and IP controls. This must be fixed before inviting more autonomous agents to commit and push.
Strongest argument against continuing with R5
R5 may be a historically attractive average of correlated, fractionally weighted backtests whose apparent smoothness cannot be reproduced with discrete futures, whose advantage over a simpler fixed ensemble is statistically unresolved, and whose expectancy disappears if a small number of future trend days fail to recur or execution worsens modestly.
Strongest argument for continuing research
The core mechanism is interpretable, the open model appears reproducible, the campaign rejected many tempting false improvements, all broad NQ ensembles remained historically positive, and the problem has been narrowed to concrete empirical questions: executability, portability, failed-persistence complementarity, and future tail recurrence.

22. Direct answers to the final questions
A. What do we actually know?
The campaign produced a plausible open directional-change engine, historically profitable NQ ensembles, strong evidence against selecting one best parameter, and a well-documented right-tail risk.
B. What did it appear to prove but not truly establish?
That R5 is superior to R4, that the volatility-control result was cleanly preregistered, that published risk metrics are true MTM, and that the ensemble is directly executable.
C. Is R5 reproducible?
Its specification is statically reconcilable. It was not independently reexecuted here.
D. Does V3/V4 equivalence hold trade-by-trade?
No. The campaign itself reports different fills and large individual-cell differences.
E. Alpha improvement, risk transformation, or lower exposure?
Unresolved mixture of alpha selection, risk transformation, and different event sampling.
F. Is the parameter region broad and stable?
R5’s reported member cluster is broader and more homogeneous than selecting one cell, but the ensemble-boundary choice still requires preregistered robustness testing.
G. Does it survive realistic costs?
It survives the reported one-tick assumption. Two ticks reportedly halve net; three may erase it. High-resolution and live-like fill confirmation remain necessary.
H. Does it preserve the right tail?
Historically yes, but the right tail is the entire system: top 1% trades contribute 160% of net.
I. Geometric growth or smooth optics?
R5’s reported Calmar and time-under-water improvements are promising, but executable and true-MTM analysis is needed before claiming improved geometric growth.
J. Confidence in DSR/PBO after the registry gap?
High confidence in the qualitative lesson that parameter selection is dangerous; low confidence in exact campaign-level selection-adjusted probabilities.
K. Simplest strong Solar architecture?
The full R4 fixed-threshold ensemble.
L. Strongest historical Solar candidate?
R5 by reported risk-adjusted point estimates; R4 remains the simpler unresolved challenger.
M. Strongest reason it may fail?
A future shortage of large directional overshoots combined with unchanged false-start and execution costs.
N. Most dangerous market regime?
Persistent low-volatility, two-way range trading with frequent threshold flips and no large trend days.
O. Highest-value unresolved Solar experiment?
Executable NQ/MNQ target-position reproduction of R5 and R4.

Failed directional change plus value/VWAP reacceptance.
