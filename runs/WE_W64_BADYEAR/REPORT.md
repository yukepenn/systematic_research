# WE_W64 — WHY 2023 WAS BAD, AND WHAT "$3,000 A WEEK" ACTUALLY MEANS · REPORT

Preregistered. Exact accounting, no backtest, nothing adopted.

**VERDICT: 2023 was bad because the market moved half as much, not because the object failed.
And the distribution behind the "$3,000/week average" is far more skewed than that phrase
suggests — that is the wave's real deliverable.**

---

**2023 had the second-highest share of TREND-UP days of any year.** The class mix was not the

## 2. `FACT` — the days were half the size

| year | available movement / session | TREND-UP available | our pts/session | capture on TREND-UP | $/wk @1.27 contracts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 319.4 | 369.9 | 11.59 | 12.37 % | $1,159 |
| **2023** | **235.2** | **272.2** | **3.04** | 18.57 % | **$302** |
| 2024 | 306.0 | 336.9 | 19.59 | 30.80 % | $1,915 |
| 2025 | 405.9 | 417.9 | 23.17 | 32.14 % | $2,265 |
| 2026 | **478.5** | **519.2** | 15.79 | 17.64 % | $1,521 |

> **2023 had the smallest available movement of any year — 235 points per session against 478
> in 2026, a factor of two.** Its capture rate (18.57 %) was mid-range, better than 2022's
> 12.37 % and 2026's 17.64 %. The object did not execute worse; there was half as much to take.

The trade-level ledger says the same thing in a different vocabulary:

| year | trades | hit % | **mean winner** | mean loser | best-5 %-of-sessions share of the year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 207 | 36.2 % | $1,172 | −$437 | 164 % |
| **2023** | **550** | **36.7 %** | **$808** | −$424 | **374 %** |
| 2024 | 595 | 36.5 % | $1,122 | −$376 | 97 % |
| 2025 | 427 | 41.2 % | $1,400 | −$503 | 98 % |
| 2026 | 163 | 39.9 % | $1,630 | −$740 | 126 % |

**Normal hit rate, normal losers, and the smallest winners of any year.** Friction is fixed in
dollars; winners are not. That is the whole mechanism, and it is the same one that explains
B-MOM's era problem (W57): a fixed cost against variable movement.

## 3. `WEAK` — was it visible while it was happening?

Trailing 60-session average available movement, lagged so it is knowable in advance:

| year | trailing available / session | realised $/wk |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 326.2 | $1,159 |
| **2023** | **246.3** | **$302** |
| 2024 | 289.9 | $1,915 |
| 2025 | 401.2 | $2,265 |
| 2026 | 451.7 | $1,521 |

The trailing measure **was** low during 2023, in real time. But:

- at the **session** level its Spearman with P&L is **+0.044**; at the **weekly** level **+0.034**.
  Essentially zero.
- the year-level correspondence rests on **five observations**, and 2026 breaks it — it has the
  highest available movement of any year and only the third-best P&L.

**So: mechanistically compelling, statistically nothing.** It explains the year after the fact
and forecasts no individual session or week. It is not a stand-aside signal and this wave does
not build one from it. (Four earlier regime-identification attempts in this campaign were
full-sample artifacts; this is how they started.)

## 4. The deliverable — what "$3,000 a week" actually means

At **2.6 contracts**, the size at which the full-window average is ~$3,000/week:

| | |
|---|---|
| **mean week** | **$3,019** |
| **median week** | **$933** |
| positive weeks | 58.3 % |
| worst week | −$15,186 |
| best week | $65,612 |
| max drawdown | $41,447 |
| longest run of losing weeks | **8** |
| **% of weeks spent under water** | **62 %** |
| **longest under-water stretch** | **29 weeks** |

And the 153 rolling 52-week windows — i.e. "what would a year have paid, starting on any week":

| percentile | 52-week average |
|---|---|
| 0th (worst year in the sample) | **−$80/wk** |
| 5th | $367/wk |
| 10th | $1,007/wk |
| 25th | $1,752/wk |
| **50th (median year)** | **$3,564/wk** |
| 75th | $4,863/wk |
| 100th | $5,881/wk |

> **38 % of one-year windows pay under $2,000/week. 10 % pay under $1,000/week. The worst pays
> −$80/week.** The mean is **3.2× the median** because a handful of enormous weeks carry it.

**That is what the average conceals, and it is the honest answer to "can we make $3,000 a week
stably".** The average is real; the word *stably* is not. On this object, at this size, you
would be below your previous equity high **62 % of the time**, and once for **29 consecutive
weeks**.

## 5. What this implies for the research, and it is not a new direction

The object's problem is not its mean — the mean is strong (annualised MAR 3.79, positive in
100 % of its 22 rolling 24-month windows). **Its problem is its SKEW: mean ÷ median = 3.2.**

And the skew is not a defect to be fixed — **it IS the edge.** A 37.8 % hit rate whose winners
carry everything (W42) and where the best 5 % of sessions deliver more than 100 % of profit
(W50) is what a trend harvester on 21 % of days looks like. Every attempt to flatten it has
failed for the same reason: W42's stops and give-back caps cut the winners; W59/W60 showed
consistency can only be bought with production at $207–$2,580 per green day.

> **Raising the MEDIAN week requires more roughly-independent bets, not a better version of this
> bet.** That is breadth, and it is the fourth independent route this campaign has taken to the
> same conclusion — W55 (no feature predicts profitability), W58 (P1 is flat on 40 % of sessions
> by design), W59/W60 (consistency is conjugate with production on one engine), and now the
> skew itself.
