SUMMARY -- what this wave establishes, and what it does not.
================================================================================================================
ESTABLISHED
  1. The opportunity did NOT shrink. In ATR units the largest session decline has been
     0.696 +/- a few percent EVERY YEAR since 2006 (PRE 0.696, MODERN 0.699) -- a flat
     line through the GFC, the 2018 vol shock, COVID and the 0DTE break. 2026 sits at
     the 63th percentile of the modern bootstrap on the full year and the 34th on the
     matched window. In POINTS 2026 is the LARGEST short opportunity in the 21-year
     record: 429 pts/session of largest decline vs a modern pool mean of 258.
  2. THE LOAD-BEARING NUMBER. Fit the sleeve's response to session geometry on
     2022-2025, feed it 2026's OWN geometry: it predicts +9.4 pts/session.
     The sleeve delivered -10.3. Residual -19.7 pts/session, block-bootstrap
     CI [-37.0, -2.8], EXCLUDES ZERO. That is the capture failure priced in points,
     and M2 already absorbs the realised drift, so it is not a headwind story.
  3. The 'headwind grew' reading does not carry the loss. On the full 2026 year the
     up-day frequency is at the 28th percentile. On the matched window it rises to the 78th --
     a real short-hostile lean, below the 90 bar, and TABLE I shows it is worth far
     less than the loss.
  4. A correction to the premise this wave was launched under. The modern drift IS
     measurable -- just not in the mean. Mean retA in MODERN has a bootstrap CI that
     spans zero, but P(up day) = 0.5361 with CI [0.5076, 0.5626], which excludes 0.50.
     The census shows why: the MEDIAN decline is ~5% smaller than the median advance
     while the MEANS are equal. Declines are RARER AND FATTER. A short wins less often
     and bigger. That is a frequency handicap, and a mean-based t-test cannot see it.

NOT ESTABLISHED / NEGATIVE
  5. G7 FAILED. The mean (advance - decline) asymmetry is NOT significant in any era.
     The short's structural handicap is a MEDIAN and FREQUENCY effect only.
  6. The retrace asymmetry (declines hand back 80% by the close, advances 55%) is
     arithmetically the up-close frequency: conditioned on an up close it is +1.03, on
     a down close it is -0.62. It is NOT an independent structural fact. Recorded as a
     control that came back negative, not as a finding.
  7. The overnight migration is real and large but NOT short-specific -- the matched
     advance control migrated with it.
  7b. HONEST CAVEAT. On the 102 sessions the sleeve actually traded in 2026, the RTH
     decline in ATR units sits at the 12th percentile -- the single
     closest any drought measure comes to its 10th-percentile bar. It does not cross it,
     the FULL-session and PF-ceiling measures on the same sessions are at the 34th and
     71th, and TABLE I prices the whole raw-material change as POSITIVE for a short.
     Two more leans belong in the same paragraph: on the full year the largest ADVANCE
     in ATR units sits at the 95th percentile while the decline sits at the 63th, and on
     the matched window the realised drift is at the 84th. The strongest honest statement is
     PREDOMINANTLY a capture failure with a mild short-hostile tilt in the raw material,
     not a pure one. TABLE I is what separates them, and it puts the tilt's value at
     +9.4 pts/session -- the WRONG SIGN to explain a loss.
  8. NO CANDIDATE IS PROPOSED. The perfect-foresight ceiling is enormous and entirely
     unreachable; 1.07% of it is realized, and a ceiling is not headroom. Nothing here
     licenses building anything.

WHAT WOULD KILL THIS
  A demonstration that the causal ATR14 normaliser is the wrong denominator -- if the
  sleeve's risk unit scales with something other than trailing session range (e.g. the
  60/460-minute sigma the Solar ratchet actually uses), then 'the opportunity is
  constant in ATR units' may not be the opportunity the sleeve can see. The point-level
  result (2026 is the largest decline year on record) does not depend on that choice.
